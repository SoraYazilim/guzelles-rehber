Every salon software vendor has learned the same lesson: put the word "free" on the button and people click. But the button and the invoice are two very different documents. If you run a hair salon or barbershop and you are shopping for booking software in 2026, the smartest thing you can do before signing anything is separate the marketing word from the business model behind it.

Four different things pretending to be one

What gets labeled "free" usually falls into one of four buckets. A time-limited trial opens everything for 7-30 days, then asks for a card. A limited freemium plan never expires but caps you at roughly 20-50 appointments a month, one staff member, and a thin slice of SMS credit. A commission model skips the subscription but shaves a cut off every booking. And a genuinely free, two-sided platform earns from connecting customers with salons rather than from the salon's wallet. Only the last one stays free as your chair fills up.

The freemium traps are worth naming out loud because they are not accidents. A 20-50 appointment ceiling is a number a single-chair shop clears in a couple of weeks, timed so that once your customer data lives inside the system, upgrading feels inevitable. Add a per-staff fee and a three-person salon pays triple. Add paid SMS credits and the "free" plan quietly drifts toward the ₺580-3,480 monthly range that paid salon software already charges.

The hidden line item nobody prices

There is a cost that never appears on any pricing page: the empty chair. Industry compilations for 2026 put the no-show rate at hair salons and barbershops somewhere in the 15-25% band, and SMS reminders cut that by 29-39%. So a plan that locks reminders behind a paid tier is not saving you money, it is handing you a no-show bill instead. Do the arithmetic on a salon taking 250 appointments a month and a 20% no-show rate turns into fifty empty slots you could have filled.
